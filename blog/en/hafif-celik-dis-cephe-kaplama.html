Light Steel House Exterior Cladding Options

The frame of a light steel house is engineered and made in a factory — but what sets the look and the external durability of the house is the exterior cladding. Render, siding, stone or panel? Here are five options compared on cost, lifespan, insulation and maintenance.

🏠

How Is a Light Steel Facade Built Up?

The layers of a light steel external wall, from inside out, are: steel stud → mineral wool inside the wall → OSB or cement-based sheathing on the outer face → water and wind barrier → and on the outside, the exterior cladding. So the cladding sits on top of a well-built insulation layer. That is why a light steel facade, done properly, can insulate better than reinforced concrete.

⚠ Important Note

A facade is not only about looks — it is the first line of defenceprotecting the house from rain, wind and heat loss. A cheap, badly applied facade brings moisture and heat problems over the years. The barrier details behind the cladding matter as much as the cladding itself.

Option 1: External Insulation with Render (EIFS)

The most common option. EPS or rock wool insulation boards are bonded to the outer sheathing, mesh and render are applied, and the surface is painted. The result: the classic rendered and painted facade plus strong insulation.

FeatureValue
Approximate cost350 – 650 TL/m²
Lifespan15 – 25 years (with repainting)
Thermal insulationVery good
MaintenancePeriodic paint and render repair
AppearanceClassic, identical to a concrete house

Who is it for? An ideal starting option for most clients who want a classic residential look and want to maximise thermal insulation.

Option 2: Fibre Cement Siding (Cement-Based Horizontal Cladding)

Cement-based horizontal panel strips with a timber texture. The signature of the modern, American-style house look. Non-combustible, rot-proof and insect-proof.

FeatureValue
Approximate cost550 – 900 TL/m²
Lifespan40 – 50 years
Thermal insulationGood (ventilated insulation behind)
MaintenanceVery little
AppearanceModern, timber textured

Who is it for? For those who want long life, low maintenance and a modern or villa aesthetic.

Option 3: Decorative Stone / Cultured Stone

Natural or cultured (manufactured) stone cladding. It gives a prestigious, durable and characterful look. It is usually applied not to the whole facade but to feature areas such as the entrance or the plinth level.

FeatureValue
Approximate cost700 – 1,400 TL/m²
Lifespan50+ years
Thermal insulationModerate (insulation behind is essential)
MaintenanceVery little
AppearancePrestigious, natural

Who is it for? For projects that want a characterful, upmarket look and have the budget for it. Often used partially, together with render.

Option 4: Composite Panel (ACP)

Flat panels with a core pressed between two aluminium faces. Common on commercial buildings and modern, minimal facades. Installed as a ventilated rainscreen system.

FeatureValue
Approximate cost800 – 1,500 TL/m²
Lifespan25 – 40 years
Thermal insulationGood (insulation plus air gap behind)
MaintenanceVery little
AppearanceModern, corporate

Who is it for? Offices, shops, modern villas; anyone wanting a sharp, corporate look. For fire safety, an A2 class (mineral core) panel should be chosen.

Option 5: Timber or Timber-Look Cladding

Natural timber or timber-look composite (WPC) boarding. It gives a warm, natural look. Natural timber needs regular maintenance; WPC needs none.

FeatureValue
Approximate cost600 – 1,200 TL/m²
LifespanNatural timber 15–25 / WPC 25–35 years
Thermal insulationModerate (insulation behind is essential)
MaintenanceHigh for natural timber / low for WPC
AppearanceWarm, natural, chalet style

Who is it for? Bungalows, chalets and summer houses; anyone after a warm, natural aesthetic.

💡 A Practical Suggestion

On most projects a combination works better than a single cladding: render on the main facade, decorative stone or timber on the entrance and feature areas. It balances the budget and enriches the look.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a light steel house be rendered so it looks like reinforced concrete?

Yes. With an EIFS system it is indistinguishable from a concrete house from the outside — you get a classic rendered, painted facade. The only difference is better thermal insulation.

Does the facade cladding affect the lifespan of the house?

Indirectly, yes. A good facade keeps water and moisture away from the frame and so protects the life of the galvanised steel. A facade with poor water protection is a source of problems in the long run. That is why the water and wind barrier behind the cladding matters at least as much as the cladding.

Can I change the facade later?

Yes. Siding, stone and panel systems can be removed and renewed. Render can also be overhauled and reapplied. The facade can be renewed without touching the frame.
